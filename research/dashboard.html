DER Integration Lab · Phase 11 · Mixed-logit choice over generated menus

Who your customers are moves the peak more than how many there are

Residential EV customers choose from a generated charging menu under a random-utility model. The same 150 vehicles, on the same hourly tariff, produce a feeder peak anywhere from 157 kW at 03:00 to 362 kW at 20:00 depending only on the preference mix of the population.

Reading

What the sweep shows

  • 01

    Price response relocates the peak, it does not remove it. A cost-driven population deserts the 19:00–21:00 window almost entirely, then re-synchronises on the overnight tariff trough and builds a fresh 125 kW spike at 03:00. The tariff is doing its job and creating a new coincidence problem at the same time.

  • 02

    Cost and battery health are aligned here, not opposed. Cost-driven customers consume the least battery capacity (about Rs 25-28 per session of pack wear, against Rs 68-87 for convenience-driven) and spend the least, because both come from choosing a lower target SOC. The real tension in the menu is cost and health on one side against range and convenience on the other.

  • 03

    Roughly half of plugged-in vehicles draw nothing. Between 43% and 62% of customers pick an option requiring no energy, because their current charge already covers tomorrow's trip plus buffer. Sizing that assumes every connected vehicle charges overstates demand substantially.

  • 04

    Arrival dispersion is the cheapest peak lever available. Moving from concentrated to dispersed plug-in times cuts the peak 24% and the coincidence factor from 0.50 to 0.36, at identical delivered energy — no tariff change and no customer persuasion required.

Method and limits

Utility is −b_cost·cost − b_wear·wear − b_delay·delay + b_soc·target with all four coefficients drawn per customer from lognormal population distributions, so signs are guaranteed and heterogeneity rather than Gumbel noise drives choice diversity.

These coefficients are illustrative research assumptions. They are not estimated from revealed- or stated-preference data, and no willingness-to-pay figure derived from them should be quoted as empirical. The tariff and EV catalogue values are likewise illustrative, not a regulated retail tariff or manufacturer specification.

Verification: raster energy matches source offer energy to 7.1×10−15 kWh; no charging appears at or after any offer's ready time across 708 offers; choice probabilities normalise to 6.7×10−16; peak load varies 2.3% across seeds. Requests are quantised to 30-minute clock, 5-point SOC and 10 km trip buckets to make the menu bank cacheable — an unquantised re-run moves peak by 4.1% and energy by 2.8%, so peak figures carry roughly that much grid sensitivity.
