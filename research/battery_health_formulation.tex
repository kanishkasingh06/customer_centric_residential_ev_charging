Session Capacity Fade and its Monetary Equivalent}
\author{}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This note states the battery-health component of the residential EV
charging menu: the semi-empirical capacity-fade model evaluated for one
connected session, its bidirectional (V2G) extension, and the mapping
from fade to rupees that makes health commensurable with the electricity
bill. Equations are numbered independently; where they correspond to the
NPSC manuscript this is noted. Every numerical claim in
Section~\ref{sec:verification} was re-derived from the parameter table
alone and agrees with the implementation to \num{1.4e-20}. Parameter
provenance and its limits are stated in Section~\ref{sec:provenance};
these are literature-anchor calibrations, not fitted coefficients, and
the monetary conversion rests on a single exogenous assumption.
\end{abstract}

\section{Session fade}

Let a connected session span the interval set $\mathcal{T} =
\{0,\dots,n-1\}$ with durations $\Delta t_k$ (hours), battery energies
$E_k$ at the $n+1$ state boundaries, and state of charge $s_k = E_k /
\Bmax$. Capacity fade over the session decomposes into three terms:
%
\begin{equation}
\dQ \;=\; \dQ^{\mathrm{cal,win}} \;+\; \dQ^{\mathrm{cal,park}}
       \;+\; \dQ^{\mathrm{cyc}} .
\label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
%
All three are dimensionless fractions of nominal usable capacity. This
replaces the normalised $0$--$100$ health score $H$ of the manuscript;
\eqref{eq:total} is a \emph{damage} measure, so the dominance direction
reverses, $\dQ_a \le \dQ_b$ where the manuscript wrote $H_a \ge H_b$.

\subsection{Calendar stress}

Calendar ageing is driven by a function of the state of charge,
%
\begin{equation}
g(s) \;=\; a_0 \;+\; a_1 s \;+\; a_2 \bigl(\max\{s - s^{\mathrm{knee}},\,0\}\bigr)^{2}
          \;+\; a_3 \bigl(s - s_c\bigr)^{3},
\label{eq:gstress}
\end{equation}
%
in fractional capacity loss per year at the reference temperature and
anchor age. Two shapes are admitted. A hinge at $s^{\mathrm{knee}}$
encodes accelerated degradation above a threshold and is flat beneath
it. A cubic centred on $s_c$ rises monotonically with a plateau at the
centre. They are not interchangeable, and which one is used decides
whether discharging a pack at moderate state of charge carries any
calendar benefit.

A parameter set may instead be fitted on cell \emph{voltage}, in which
case
%
\begin{equation}
g(s) \;=\; \kappa\,\bigl(\mu\, V(s) + \nu\bigr),
\label{eq:gvolt}
\end{equation}
%
with $V(s)$ read off a tabulated OCV curve. The two forms are
alternatives, not components: a parameter set declares one or the other.

\paragraph{LFP.} The coefficients are those of Naumann et al.\
(2018)~\cite{naumann2018}, converted to the differential form of
Section~\ref{sec:parameters}. That study reports a cubic,
$a_1 = a_2 = 0$ with $s_c = 0.5$, and calendar loss rising
monotonically with state of charge by a factor of \num{3.915} from
empty to full. An earlier version of this formulation used a hinge at
$s^{\mathrm{knee}} = 0.70$ with $a_1 = 0$, which is flat below
\SI{70}{\percent} and gives a full-to-empty spread of only
\num{1.24}; it is not what the cited study measured and has been
replaced.

\paragraph{NMC.} Schmalstieg et al.~\cite{schmalstieg2014} fit calendar
ageing linearly on cell voltage, so \eqref{eq:gvolt} applies with
$\mu = \num{7.543}$, $\nu = \num{-23.75}$ and $V(s)$ from their Fig.~1,
digitised at \SI{1}{\percent} intervals. Their Eq.~(14) is cumulative in
days; converting to the per-year incremental form used here gives
%
\begin{equation}
\kappa \;=\; 0.75 \cdot 365^{0.75} \cdot 10^{6} \cdot
  \exp\!\left(-6976 / T^{\mathrm{ref}}\right) \;=\; \num{0.0043184}.
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
%
The linear term crosses zero at $V = \SI{3.1486}{\volt}$; the digitised
curve bottoms at \SI{3.3346}{\volt}, so calendar stress stays positive
across the whole range with no floor required.

Two multiplicative factors move $g$ off its reference point. Temperature
follows Arrhenius,
%
\begin{equation}
\theta(T) \;=\; \exp\!\left[-\frac{E_a}{R}
  \left(\frac{1}{T} - \frac{1}{T^{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)\right],
\qquad T,\,T^{\mathrm{ref}} \text{ in kelvin},
\label{eq:arrhenius}
\end{equation}
%
with $R = \SI{8.314462618}{\joule\per\mole\per\kelvin}$. Ageing is a
sublinear power law in calendar time, $Q \propto t^{\alpha}$ with
$\alpha \in (0,1]$, so the \emph{local} rate at pack age $t$ relative to
the anchor age $t_{0}$ is the ratio of derivatives,
%
\begin{equation}
\psi(t) \;=\;
  \frac{\alpha\, t^{\alpha-1}}{\alpha\, t_{0}^{\alpha-1}}
  \;=\; \left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}\right)^{\alpha-1}.
\label{eq:agefactor}
\end{equation}
%
Since $\alpha < 1$, $\psi$ decreases with age: an older pack fades more
slowly per unit time. The model is therefore incremental, charging each
session the marginal rate at the pack's current age rather than an
average, which is the correct treatment for a power law.

The anchor age $t_{0}$ is a normalisation, not a physical quantity. It
fixes the age at which $g(s)$ in \eqref{eq:gstress} is literally the
fractional loss per year, so that $\psi(t_{0}) = 1$ by construction.
Taking $t_{0} = \SI{1}{\yr}$ reduces \eqref{eq:agefactor} to
$t^{\alpha-1}$ with $t$ in years and places the coefficients near the
one-to-two-year span of the calendar-ageing campaigns they are drawn
from; the singularity at $t\to 0$ rules out anchoring at zero.
Consequently $t_{0}$ and $(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)$ are \emph{not} independent:
re-anchoring to $t_0'$ requires scaling every $a_i$ by
$(t_0'/t_{0})^{1-\alpha}$, failing which the model changes silently.
For $t_0' = \SI{2}{\yr}$ that factor is \num{1.189} (NMC) and
\num{1.414} (LFP).

\subsection{Charging-window and parked-day calendar fade}

Over the connected session, stress is accumulated at the left endpoint
of each interval:
%
\begin{equation}
\dQ^{\mathrm{cal,win}} \;=\; \theta(T)\,\psi(t)
  \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} g\!\left(\min\{\max\{s_k,0\},1\}\right)
  \frac{\Delta t_k}{H_{\mathrm{yr}}},
  \qquad H_{\mathrm{yr}} = \num{8760}.
\label{eq:calwin}
\end{equation}
%
The remainder of the day is spent parked at the delivered state of
charge, less the energy consumed by the trip $E^{\mathrm{trip}}$:
%
\begin{equation}
s^{\mathrm{park}} \;=\;
  \frac{\max\{\zeta,\,z\}\,\Bmax - E^{\mathrm{trip}}}{\Bmax},
\qquad
\dQ^{\mathrm{cal,park}} \;=\; g\!\left(s^{\mathrm{park}}\right)
  \theta(T)\,\psi(t)\,\frac{H_{\mathrm{park}}}{H_{\mathrm{yr}}},
\label{eq:calpark}
\end{equation}
%
where $z$ is the requested target, $\zeta$ the declared departure floor,
and $H_{\mathrm{park}}$ the parked hours. Because terminal energy is
pinned by equality to $\max\{\zeta,z\}\Bmax$,
\eqref{eq:calpark} is constant at fixed $(z,\zeta)$ and independent of
the charging schedule. This is Proposition~1: the parked term is a
constant of the request and may be excluded from the trajectory
objective without changing its argument minimum.

\subsection{Cycle fade}

Cycle ageing is a power law in accumulated equivalent full cycles $N$,
modulated by depth of discharge and rate. Its local slope is
%
\begin{equation}
\sigma(N) \;=\; \beta \,\max\{N,\,N^{\min}\}^{\,\beta-1},
\label{eq:cycslope}
\end{equation}
%
with $N^{\min}>0$ a regularisation floor for the singular derivative at
$N=0$. Then
%
\begin{equation}
\dQ^{\mathrm{cyc}} \;=\; \Gamma \,
  \bigl(1 + c_C\,\hat{C}\bigr)\,
  \sigma(N)\;\varphi ,
\qquad
\Gamma =
\begin{cases}
c_{\mathrm{ref}}\bigl(1 + c_D \delta\bigr) & \text{multiplicative,}\\[2pt]
c_{\mathrm{ref}} + c_D \delta + c_{D3}\bigl(\delta - \delta_c\bigr)^{3}
  + c_V\bigl(\bar{V} - V_c\bigr)^{2} & \text{additive,}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:cyc}
\end{equation}
%
where $\varphi$ is battery-side throughput as a fraction of capacity,
$\delta$ the state-of-charge excursion, $\hat{C}$ the peak C-rate and
$\bar{V}$ the mean cell voltage of the session. The additive branch is
the shape Schmalstieg et al.~\cite{schmalstieg2014} Eq.~(20) fits. Its
mean-voltage term penalises cycling about a high state of charge, which
is where V2G operates, so omitting it understates bidirectional
cycling. Their coefficients multiply $\sqrt{Q}$ with $Q$ the charge
throughput in ampere-hours, so each is scaled by
$\sqrt{2C_{\mathrm{nom}}}/2 = \num{1.012423}$ for their
\SI{2.05}{\ampere\hour} cell to express fade per equivalent full cycle.
$\varphi$, $\delta$ and $\hat{C}$ are where the bidirectional extension
enters.

\section{Bidirectional extension}
\label{sec:v2g}

Write $E^{\mathrm{in}}_k \ge 0$ and $E^{\mathrm{out}}_k \ge 0$ for grid
import and export in interval $k$. Battery energy evolves as
%
\begin{equation}
E_{k+1} \;=\; E_k \;+\; \etain E^{\mathrm{in}}_k
  \;-\; \frac{E^{\mathrm{out}}_k}{\etaout}.
\label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
%
Throughput counts both directions on the battery side:
%
\begin{equation}
\varphi \;=\; \frac{1}{\Bmax}
  \left( \etain \sum_{k} E^{\mathrm{in}}_k
       + \frac{1}{\etaout}\sum_{k} E^{\mathrm{out}}_k \right),
\qquad
\Delta N \;=\; \frac{\varphi}{2}.
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
%
The equivalent-full-cycle convention in \eqref{eq:phi} is a definition,
not an inheritance: one EFC is $2\Bmax$ of battery-side throughput, so a
full charge followed by a full discharge advances $N$ by exactly one.

Under charge-only operation the state of charge is monotone, and
throughput coincides with the state-of-charge swing, so $\varphi$ serves
as a depth proxy. Export breaks that, and depth must be stated
explicitly:
%
\begin{equation}
\delta \;=\; \max_{k} s_k \;-\; \min_{k} s_k .
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
%
\begin{proposition}[backward compatibility]
If $E^{\mathrm{out}}_k \equiv 0$ and no trip occurs within the session,
then $\delta = \varphi$ exactly.
\end{proposition}
%
\noindent
Peak C-rate likewise takes the larger of the two directions:
%
\begin{equation}
\hat{C} \;=\; \frac{1}{\Bmax} \max_{k}
  \max\!\left\{
    \frac{\etain E^{\mathrm{in}}_k}{\Delta t_k},\;
    \lambda_d\,\frac{E^{\mathrm{out}}_k}{\etaout \Delta t_k}
  \right\}.
\label{eq:chat}
\end{equation}
%
Here $\lambda_d$ is a discharge-rate severity multiplier. It has no
anchor in the parameter sources used here, neither of which reports a
discharge-side C-rate coefficient, and is therefore exposed as a
sensitivity parameter rather than claimed as calibrated.

Two structural consequences follow. First, $\varphi$ becomes a decision
variable under export, so \eqref{eq:cyc} can no longer be treated as a
constant of the request and must re-enter the trajectory objective.
Second, $\bigl(1 + c_D\delta\bigr)\bigl(1 + c_C\hat{C}\bigr)\varphi$ is
not convex, so convexity claims valid for the charge-only problem do not
survive. A claim made in an earlier version of this formulation is
\emph{withdrawn} here. It held that, because LFP calendar ageing was
flat below the knee, export from beneath it carried no calendar
benefit. That rested on the hinge form; under the cubic actually
reported by Naumann et al.\ \cite{naumann2018}, $g$ is strictly
increasing in $s$ everywhere, so lowering the state of charge does
reduce calendar ageing at every operating point. The quantitative
claim is modest --- moving a pack from \SI{65}{\percent} to
\SI{45}{\percent} reduces $g$ by roughly a quarter --- and it is
offset by the cycle fade the export itself incurs, so no headline
claim that V2G improves battery health follows either way.

\section{Monetary equivalent}
\subsection{Pricing a session}

The menu offers four attributes to a customer who must trade battery
wear against the electricity bill. A normalised health index does not
support that trade; a price does. Since $\dQ$ is the \emph{fraction} of
usable capacity consumed, multiplying by capacity and pack cost gives
rupees of battery spent:
%
\begin{equation}
W \;=\; \dQ \,\cdot\, \Bmax \,\cdot\, \pi_{\mathrm{chem}},
\label{eq:wear}
\end{equation}
%
with $\pi_{\mathrm{chem}}$ the chemistry-specific pack cost in
\si{\rupee\per\kilo\watt\hour}. Wear and bill are then in the same unit,
and the only free quantity in the choice model is their ratio: how much
of a rupee of consumed pack a customer treats as a rupee out of pocket.

\subsection{What to display}

Equation~\eqref{eq:wear} prices the whole session, which is the correct
quantity for the choice model and the wrong one to put in front of a
customer. The reason is that a large part of $W$ is incurred whichever
offer is taken. The parked-day term \eqref{eq:calpark} depends only on
the delivered state of charge and the trip, so it accrues even when the
customer declines to charge altogether; in the worked example of
Section~\ref{sec:verification} it is the largest of the three
components, at \SI{43}{\percent} of total fade. A customer comparing two
rows of a menu is choosing between them, not between charging and
owning a battery, so the figure shown against an offer should be the
wear its selection \emph{adds}.

Let $\dQ_0$ be the smallest total fade among the offers of one menu. In
practice the minimiser is the decline-to-charge option, so $\dQ_0$ is
the fade the customer incurs by doing nothing. For an offer of fade
$\dQ$ and saving $S$, define
%
\begin{equation}
W^{\mathrm{marg}} \;=\; \bigl(\dQ - \dQ_0\bigr)\Bmax\,\pi_{\mathrm{chem}},
\qquad
\mathrm{Net} \;=\; S - W^{\mathrm{marg}}.
\label{eq:net}
\end{equation}
%
$W^{\mathrm{marg}}$ is the pack spend attributable to the decision and
$\mathrm{Net}$ is what the offer is worth once that spend is paid for.
Three properties of \eqref{eq:net} should travel with any figure
computed from it.

It is a presentation convention, not part of the model. The choice
model uses absolute $W$ from \eqref{eq:wear}. Within one request the two
differ by the constant $\dQ_0 \Bmax \pi_{\mathrm{chem}}$, which cancels
in a logit and so leaves choice probabilities unchanged; across requests
it does not cancel, and the two conventions must not be mixed.

$\dQ_0$ is a property of the menu, not of the vehicle. Regenerate the
menu with a different tier ladder and $\dQ_0$ moves, so
$\mathrm{Net}$ ranks offers within one menu and is not comparable
across menus or across sessions.

$\mathrm{Net}$ can be negative, and this is the point of computing it.
An offer whose saving is smaller than the pack it consumes destroys
value; in the worked menu of Section~\ref{sec:verification} two of
fifteen displayed offers are negative, which is not visible when the
health column reports a raw fade fraction.

\section{Provenance and limitations}
\label{sec:provenance}

\paragraph{LFP calendar: reproduced, not approximated.}
The LFP calendar coefficients are Table~7 of Naumann et
al.~\cite{naumann2018}, converted as stated in
Table~\ref{tab:params}. The implementation reproduces that paper's own
published projection --- \SI{19.0}{\percent} capacity loss at 20 years,
\SI{25}{\celsius}, \SI{50}{\percent} SOC --- as \SI{19.01}{\percent},
and agrees with their Eq.~(3) to a relative \num{1e-6} across
temperatures \SIrange{0}{60}{\celsius}, all states of charge and
horizons from six months to twenty years. Both checks are asserted in
the test suite, so a later edit to a coefficient breaks a test rather
than quietly invalidating the citation.

\paragraph{NMC: Schmalstieg et al.\ (2014), reproduced.}
All NMC coefficients are from Schmalstieg et al.~\cite{schmalstieg2014},
a Sanyo UR18650E study of more than sixty cells covering calendar and
cycle ageing. The calendar term is their Eq.~(14) in its published
voltage form, with $V(s)$ from Fig.~1 digitised at \SI{1}{\percent}
intervals; one year at \SI{25}{\celsius} and \SI{50}{\percent} SOC
gives \SI{2.395}{\percent} against the \SI{2.390}{\percent} their
equation gives directly, the residual being digitisation. The OCV curve
reproduces both cell voltages their text states, \SI{3.7000}{\volt}
against \num{3.699} at \SI{50}{\percent} SOC and \SI{4.1596}{\volt}
against \num{4.162} at full, which is $+\SI{0.18}{\percent}$ and
$-\SI{0.24}{\percent}$ in their ageing factor. The time exponent
$\alpha = 0.75$ is their Section~3.2 choice, adopted after a $\sqrt{t}$
law fitted their data poorly. The activation energy follows from their
exponent $\exp(-6976/T)$, giving
$E_a = 6976R = \SI{58002}{\joule\per\mole}$ (the paper, rounding $R$ to \num{8.314}, prints \SI{58.0}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}; the stored value reproduces their \emph{exponent} under CODATA $R$). The cycle terms are
their Eq.~(20) in the additive branch of \eqref{eq:cyc}, including the
mean-voltage term, with $\beta = 0.5$ from their Eq.~(18).

Two earlier values are \emph{superseded}. An activation energy of
\SI{24}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} understated temperature sensitivity by
\num{2.42} and the \SIrange{25}{40}{\celsius} acceleration by
\num{1.93}; it most plausibly came from the
\SI{24.5}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} empirical fit of
Wang/Purewal~\cite{wang2014}, a different cell. A depth coefficient of
unity understated depth sensitivity \num{5.37}-fold against the ratio
their Eq.~(20) prints.

\paragraph{A citation error, corrected.}
An earlier version of this work cited ``Schmalstieg et al.\ (2014)''
against the title of Wang/Purewal et al.~\cite{wang2014,purewal2014},
conflating two distinct 2014 \emph{J.\ Power Sources} papers. Purewal
et al.\ is a physics-based chemical--mechanical model at fixed
\SI{50}{\percent} depth of discharge, with no state-of-charge stress
function, a $\sqrt{t}$ calendar term, and cycle ageing as Paris'-law
fatigue crack growth ($m = 2.2$). None of it is used here; it is kept
in the bibliography only because its Part~1 companion is the likely
origin of the superseded activation energy.

\paragraph{LFP cycle: Naumann et al.\ (2020), reproduced.}
The companion cycle study~\cite{naumann2020} has since appeared: same
Sony/Murata cell, 19 test points over 885 days. Its Eq.~(5),
$Q^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{\mathrm{loss}} = k_T k_{C} k_{\mathrm{DOC}}
\mathrm{FEC}^{0.5}$, supplies every LFP cycle coefficient here. The
C-rate factor of their Eq.~(8), $k_C = 0.0630\,C + 0.0971$, normalised
on its intercept gives $c_C = \num{0.648816}$ --- the coefficient that
had no empirical support anywhere before this paper, and which the NMC
branch still lacks because Schmalstieg et al.\ ran every cycle test at
1C. The depth factor of their Eq.~(10) is \emph{cubic} in depth of
cycle centred on \num{0.6}, not linear, which is why \eqref{eq:cyc}
carries a $c_{D3}$ term. Both depth terms scale by $0.0971/200$ to give
fade per equivalent full cycle. Their Section~3.2.1 reports the
temperature influence on pure cycle ageing between \SI{25}{\celsius}
and \SI{40}{\celsius} as negligible, so their model carries no cycle
temperature term and neither does this one.

One caveat travels with it, and it is the authors'\ own. Test points
below \SI{80}{\percent} depth showed unsteady, partly reversible
capacity loss whose mechanism they state is not understood, so
$k_{\mathrm{DOC}}$ was fitted on the \SI{80}{\percent} and
\SI{100}{\percent} points alone. Shallow cycling is therefore the
least trustworthy region of this parameterisation, and V2G arbitrage
sits squarely in it.

\paragraph{Temperature.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:arrhenius} propagates strongly. Pack temperature is
held at a scenario constant here; fade precision beyond what that
assumption supports should not be quoted.

\paragraph{Multi-day coupling.}
$N$ is a scenario input and is not advanced between sessions in the
charge-only results. This is benign where $\varphi$ is small and
$\sigma$ slowly varying, and degrades under V2G, where per-session
throughput is several times larger.

\paragraph{Asymmetric regularisation of the two power laws.}
The cycle slope \eqref{eq:cycslope} is floored at $N^{\min}$, bounding
$\sigma \le \beta (N^{\min})^{\beta-1} = \num{0.5}$ for a pack with no
accumulated throughput. The age factor \eqref{eq:agefactor} carries no
counterpart: $\psi$ grows without bound as $t \to 0$
($\psi = \num{1.78}$ at \SI{0.1}{\yr}, \num{3.16} at \SI{0.01}{\yr} for
NMC) and only a non-positive age is rejected. Both expressions are
singular derivatives of the same power-law form, so the absence of a
floor on one is an inconsistency rather than a modelling choice. Results
for near-new packs should be treated with care until an explicit minimum
anchor age is introduced.

\paragraph{Depth measure.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:delta} uses the full-session excursion as the depth
proxy. Validation against rainflow counting is outstanding and should
compare \emph{offer rankings} rather than absolute fade.

\section{Unresolved}
\label{sec:unresolved}

Collected here rather than scattered, in rough order of how much they
could move a result.

\paragraph{1. The costing convention undercharges, and the price
behind it is the wrong kind of price.}
Two separable problems.

\emph{Convention.} Equation~\eqref{eq:wear} prices the \emph{fraction
of capacity lost}. The V2G optimisation literature instead writes the
\emph{whole pack off} over the throughput it survives: Preis and
Biedenbach~\cite{preis2023} review the field and find two usable
forms, a weighted Ah-throughput model with a constant cost factor
$\mathrm{CF} = \pi B^{\max} / E^{\mathrm{EoL}}$ and a performance-based
piecewise model, with end of life at \SI{20}{\percent} capacity loss.
At equal fade the two conventions differ by exactly that threshold:
\eqref{eq:wear} charges \num{0.2} of what the throughput form charges,
because reaching end of life costs the owner a pack, not a fifth of
one. On that axis this formulation \emph{under}charges fivefold.

\emph{Price.} $\pi_{\mathrm{chem}}$ is an OEM procurement price for new
capacity with no end-of-life divisor in it. What an owner loses is
retail replacement, which carries labour, margin and tax; or
resale-value loss, since capacity is realised in money only at
replacement or sale and not in arbitrarily small increments; or, inside
an \num{8}-year warranty, close to nothing at the margin.
Hein et al.~\cite{hein2012} model the residual value of used EV packs
endogenously and obtain roughly \SI{18}{\euro\per\kilo\watt\hour},
an order of magnitude under new-capacity price.

\emph{Net effect, measured.} For the highest-saving offer of the worked
menu (\SI{53.7}{\kilo\watt\hour} battery-side throughput), this
formulation gives \SI{1.57}{\rupee\per\kilo\watt\hour} of throughput
against the \SIrange{0.24}{0.47}{\rupee\per\kilo\watt\hour} implied by
\cite{preis2023}'s published \SIrange{0.23}{0.45}{\eurocent\per\kilo\watt\hour}.
So the two errors run opposite ways and do not cancel: the convention
is fivefold low, while the fade model is fast enough to more than
offset it --- its cycle term implies end of life at about \num{1500}
equivalent full cycles, which is pessimistic for NMC. The figures here
land three to six times above the published band.

\emph{What would fix it.} Restate $W$ as
$\mathrm{CF}\cdot Q$ with $\mathrm{CF}$ derived through an explicit
end-of-life divisor, and calibrate against~\cite{preis2023} rather than
against a pack price alone. Their methodological finding also bears on
the choice: the constant-factor form is the better optimisation
formulation, because the state-of-health-dependent piecewise model
moves average degradation cost \emph{by a factor of \num{9.71}} when
initial SoH shifts from \SI{95}{\percent} to \SI{100}{\percent}. This
formulation already uses a constant factor, which is the right side of
that result.

\paragraph{2. Shallow-cycle fade is substantially \emph{reversible},
and V2G sits in exactly that region.}
Naumann et al.~\cite{naumann2020} fit their depth factor on the
\SI{80}{\percent} and \SI{100}{\percent} points only, because
shallower cycling showed unsteady loss they could not explain. The
follow-up study~\cite{spingler2020} explains it, and the explanation
matters more than the uncertainty did.

Cycling anywhere between \SI{30}{\percent} and \SI{70}{\percent} SOC
--- where the LFP equilibrium voltage curve is flat --- produces
\emph{larger} capacity losses than full cycles, up to
\SI{21}{\percent} after 1000 FEC against \SI{6}{\percent} for full
cycles. But most of it is not degradation. Holding the cell at
\SI{0}{\percent} or \SI{100}{\percent} SOC recovers it: after 35 days
at \SI{2}{\volt}, \SI{89}{\percent} of the lost capacity returned.
Post-mortem analysis attributes the loss to non-uniform lithium
distribution across the electrodes rather than lithium inventory loss,
and the same cells after full cycling showed almost no recoverable
capacity.

Two consequences, pulling opposite ways. Pricing shallow V2G cycling
at the \cite{naumann2020} rate \emph{overstates} permanent damage,
because a large part of the measured loss is recoverable. But the
capacity is genuinely unavailable to the customer until a recovery
hold is performed, and no residential charging schedule will park a
car at \SI{0}{\percent} SOC for a week. So the fade is neither as
permanent as the cycle model implies nor as harmless as reversibility
suggests, and this formulation represents neither state. Any V2G
result for an LFP pack cycling in the \SIrange{30}{70}{\percent}
window should say so.

\paragraph{2b. The two LFP cycle studies disagree with each other.}
Wang et al.~\cite{wang2011} fit a graphite-LiFePO$_4$ cycle model on
an A123 \SI{2.2}{\ampere\hour} cell over
\SIrange{-30}{60}{\celsius} and C/2 to 10C, and reach two conclusions
opposite to~\cite{naumann2020} on the Sony/Murata cell. They find
depth of discharge has \emph{negligible} effect on capacity fade,
where~\cite{naumann2020} fit a cubic spanning \num{6.06} from zero to
full depth. And they find a strong Arrhenius temperature dependence on
cycle ageing, $E_a = \SI{31.5}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}$,
where~\cite{naumann2020} report the influence between
\SI{25}{\celsius} and \SI{40}{\celsius} as negligible and carry no
cycle temperature term at all. The two agree only on the throughput
exponent, $z \approx 0.55$ against \num{0.5}.

Both are credible studies on commercial LFP cells. The implication is
that the LFP cycle parameterisation adopted here is not robust across
cell designs, and that the depth and temperature terms in particular
should be treated as cell-specific rather than chemistry-specific.
\cite{wang2011} also supplies a C-rate mechanism this formulation does
not represent: their activation energy falls linearly with rate,
$E_a = 31700 - 370.3\,C$, so rate and temperature interact rather than
multiplying independently.

\paragraph{3. The NMC C-rate coefficient still has no support.}
Schmalstieg et al.\ ran every cycle test at 1C and \SI{35}{\celsius},
so $c_C = 0$ for NMC and $\lambda_d$ stays a swept sensitivity
parameter. LFP is now sourced from~\cite{naumann2020}; NMC is not, and
the two chemistries therefore differ in whether this term exists at
all.

\paragraph{4. The two chemistries no longer share a functional form.}
Both now use the additive branch, but LFP's depth term is cubic in
$\delta$ centred on \num{0.6} while NMC's is linear, LFP's calendar
term is a cubic in state of charge while NMC's is linear in voltage,
and only NMC carries a mean-voltage cycle term. Each follows its own
source faithfully; cross-chemistry comparisons carry all four
differences.

\paragraph{5. No multi-day coupling.}
$N$ is a scenario input, never advanced between sessions. Benign where
$\varphi$ is small and $\sigma$ slowly varying; degrades under V2G,
where per-session throughput is several times larger.

\paragraph{6. Temperature is a scenario constant.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:arrhenius} propagates strongly, and more so now that
$E_a$ for NMC is \SI{58}{\kilo\joule\per\mole} rather than
\num{24}: a \SI{10}{\celsius} error moves calendar fade by about a
factor of two. No fade precision beyond that assumption is supportable.

\paragraph{7. The age factor has no regularisation floor.}
$\sigma(N)$ is floored at $N^{\min}$; $\psi(t)$ is not, and grows
without bound as $t \to 0$. Both are singular derivatives of the same
power-law form, so this is an inconsistency rather than a choice.

\paragraph{8. $\delta$ is unvalidated as a depth measure.}
Rainflow comparison is outstanding and should compare offer
\emph{rankings}, not absolute fade. Li et al.~\cite{li2020} apply
rainflow cycle counting inside a V2G scheduling optimisation at a
\SI{15}{\minute} control interval, on the argument that conventional
fade models resolve days or weeks where V2G scheduling needs minutes
to hours, which is a usable precedent for the protocol. Their
degradation objective is a bare cycle count with no fade model and no
cost, so nothing is calibrated from it.

\paragraph{9. The OCV curve is digitised, and single-cell.}
Two points required a \SI{0.1}{\milli\volt} monotonicity repair. More
importantly it is one cell's curve at \SI{35}{\celsius}, applied to
every NMC pack regardless of size, age or temperature; OCV shifts with
all three.

\paragraph{10. Both parameter sets are single-cell, single-study.}
A \SI{3}{\ampere\hour} 26650 LFP cell and a \SI{2.05}{\ampere\hour}
18650 NMC cell, each from one laboratory campaign, stand in for every
pack of their chemistry. Cell-to-cell and pack-level variation is not
represented.
